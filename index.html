Welcome to the Rane Lab at Columbia University. We build mechanistic frameworks to understand B and T cell dynamics across developmental, homeostatic, and response timelines.

By integrating physics/ecology-driven principles, deep generative inference (VAEs, Transformers), and experimental multiomics/imaging, we model continuous cell-state transitions and spatial interactions.

Research Focus

Generative Inference

Deep Generative Inference & Trajectory Optimization
To move beyond simple discrete-compartment models, we develop continuous representations of cell states using time-conditioned Variational Autoencoders (VAEs) and Itô stochastic differential equations (SDEs). Furthermore, our framework—viaABC (Variational Inference-assisted Approximate Bayesian Computation)—distills high-dimensional spatiotemporal imaging and single-cell timeseries to fit and select mechanistic drift-diffusion models.

Lymphocyte Dynamics

Lymphocyte Development & Homeostasis
Using Cre-lox lineage tracing, fate-reporter mouse systems, and mathematical modeling, we quantify T and B cell regulation:

  • Naive T Cell Lifespan: Post-thymic cell age dictates life expectancy, challenging classical feedback regulation models.
  • Treg Lineage Mapping: Uncovering a linear pathway of regulatory T cell (Treg) development from thymic origin to memory and tumor-infiltrating subsets.
  • B Cell Homeostasis & Activation: Identifying extrinsic driver rules governing aging immunoglobulin repertoires and activation-triggered marginal zone B cell fates.

Enhancing Antibody-mediated Tumor Immunity

Clone-Constrained Transcriptional Trajectories
Using advanced computational methods, we analyze the transcriptional dynamics of B and T cells in response in tumors. We combine language transformers, VAEs, and dual-labeling reporter mouse models to map B cell activation histories and clonal divergence in tumors. Our models integrate paired single-cell RNA and V(D)J sequencing data to infer differentiation trajectories constrained by clonal trees and temporal activation windows. The ultimate goal is to identify molecular programs and gene regulatory networks that enhance antibody-mediated tumor immunity.

Principal Investigator

Sanket Rane, PhD is an immunologist turned mathematical modeler, trained in deterministic and probabilistic mathematical modeling, machine learning, Bayesian inference, and cell biology. Sanket earned his PhD in immunology at the National Institute of Immunology in India, investigating molecular and cellular factors governing T cell kinetics during healthy aging. He pivoted to using computational approaches to study lymphocyte ecology during his postdoctoral years at the University of Glasgow and Columbia University. His overarching interest lies in understanding the origins of variability in immune responses, particularly the mechanisms that shape antibody-mediated responses to vaccines, infections, and cancer.
